# 04 – Execution Playbook / SOP

> ## Why this document exists
>
> Documents 01–03 established the research methodology, the strategic decisions, and the operating model behind this playbook.
>
> This document converts those decisions into a repeatable execution process.
>
> It answers a practical question:
>
> **"If I joined a B2B SaaS company tomorrow, how would I execute this strategy consistently?"**
>
> This is intentionally written as an operating playbook rather than a checklist.
>
> Every SOP explains not only **what** should be done, but also **why** I believe it should be done, what evidence influenced that decision, which alternatives I rejected, and where the approach may fail.

---

# How to Use This Playbook

This playbook is designed to be followed sequentially.

Every SOP assumes that the previous one has already been completed.

Skipping a step increases the likelihood of producing content that is disconnected from customer problems, inconsistent with company positioning or difficult to improve over time.

The execution flow is intentionally simple.

```
Customer Problem
        ↓
Content Opportunity
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Creation
        ↓
Review
        ↓
Distribution
        ↓
Measurement
        ↓
Learning
```

Every stage has a defined objective, decision gate and expected outcome.

The objective is consistency rather than speed.

# SOP 01 — Selecting the Right Content Opportunity

## Objective

Identify content opportunities that solve meaningful customer problems rather than simply filling the publishing calendar.

The quality of every subsequent stage depends on the quality of this decision.

Choosing the wrong topic cannot usually be corrected by stronger writing or better distribution.

For that reason, I believe topic selection deserves significantly more attention than it typically receives.

---

## My Decision

I decided that every content idea should originate from **validated customer problems** rather than internal brainstorming sessions.

The research consistently showed that the strongest content begins with customer understanding.

Content should therefore answer questions customers are already asking—not questions the marketing team assumes are important.

Not AI.

Customers.

Because this recommendation appeared across multiple independent experts working in different disciplines, I considered it sufficiently strong to become the first execution rule of this playbook.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some organisations begin content planning with:

- seasonal campaigns
- company announcements
- product releases
- trending industry topics

These inputs are valuable.

However, I found little evidence that they consistently outperform content driven by recurring customer problems.

---

## Why I Rejected The Alternative

I deliberately chose not to build the execution process around campaign calendars.

Campaigns are temporary.

Customer problems are recurring.

When content consistently answers recurring customer questions, it remains useful long after a campaign has ended.

That makes it a stronger long-term investment.

---

## Execution Steps

### Step 1 — Collect Customer Signals

Gather insights from multiple sources.

Examples include:

- Sales call recordings
- Customer interviews
- Customer Success conversations
- Support tickets
- Product feedback
- LinkedIn comments
- Community discussions
- CRM notes

Avoid rewriting customer language at this stage.

Capture questions exactly as customers ask them.

---

### Step 2 — Identify Recurring Problems

Group similar observations together.

Ignore one-off requests.

Prioritise problems that repeatedly appear across multiple conversations.

The objective is identifying patterns rather than isolated comments.

---

### Step 3 — Evaluate Business Relevance

Before approving an idea, answer the following questions.

| Decision Gate | Yes / No |
|---------------|----------|
| Does this solve a genuine customer problem? | □ |
| Has this problem appeared repeatedly? | □ |
| Does it strengthen our positioning? | □ |
| Can we contribute an original perspective? | □ |
| Would the content remain valuable six months from now? | □ |

If any answer is **No**, the topic returns to the research backlog.

---

## Quality Gate

A content idea is ready for production only when:

- the customer problem is clearly defined;
- supporting evidence exists from multiple conversations or observations;
- the proposed topic aligns with the company's positioning;
- there is a clear educational outcome;
- the team can contribute an original point of view.

---

## Common Failure Modes

The following issues appeared repeatedly during my research and would prevent a topic from moving forward:

- Creating content because "it's time to post."
- Following trends unrelated to customer needs.
- Confusing product announcements with educational content.
- Choosing topics already saturated without adding a differentiated perspective.
- Allowing AI to suggest topics without validating them against customer evidence.

---

## Success Criteria

This SOP has been executed successfully when:

- Every approved content idea is traceable to a real customer problem.
- The content backlog reflects customer priorities rather than internal assumptions.
- Marketing, Sales and Customer Success share a common understanding of customer challenges.
- The team spends less time searching for ideas because research continuously feeds the content pipeline.

---

## My Notes

This is the most important SOP in the entire playbook.

If the wrong problem is selected, every subsequent stage becomes an optimisation exercise around the wrong objective.

A strong operating system therefore begins by making better decisions before creating better content.

That principle influenced the design of every remaining SOP in this repository.

# SOP 02 — Validating the Content Opportunity Before Writing

## Objective

Confirm that a content idea deserves investment before time is spent writing, designing or distributing it.

One observation became increasingly clear during my research:

Poor execution rarely begins with poor writing.

It usually begins with choosing an idea that was never strong enough to justify execution.

This SOP exists to eliminate weak ideas before production starts.

---

## My Decision

I decided that every content opportunity should pass a structured validation process before entering production.

The objective is not to eliminate creativity.

The objective is to ensure that creative effort is invested in problems that matter to the audience and the business.

Validation should therefore focus on customer relevance, strategic alignment and differentiation rather than personal preference.

This convinced me that idea validation should become a formal decision point rather than an informal discussion.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some teams rely primarily on intuition when selecting content ideas.

Common decision criteria include:

- "This topic feels interesting."
- "Our competitors are posting about it."
- "This trend is getting attention."
- "The founder wants to write about it."

These approaches occasionally produce successful content.

However, they introduce significant inconsistency because they are difficult to evaluate objectively.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I deliberately avoided intuition-driven content selection because it becomes difficult to repeat consistently across different team members.

A repeatable operating model should produce similar decisions regardless of who is creating the content.

For that reason, I replaced subjective judgement with a structured validation process.

---

## Execution Steps

### Step 1 — Define the Customer Problem

Document the exact customer problem the content intends to solve.

Avoid broad statements such as:

> "Explain AI."

Instead define the problem precisely.

Example:

> "Help B2B SaaS marketing leaders understand where AI should and should not replace human judgement."

Specific problems produce stronger content.

---

### Step 2 — Define the Learning Outcome

Every content asset should teach the audience something they did not understand before.

Complete the following statement:

> **After reading this content, the audience should be able to...**

If this sentence cannot be completed clearly, the topic requires further refinement.

---

### Step 3 — Identify the Original Contribution

Every approved topic should contribute at least one element that competitors are unlikely to publish.

Examples include:

- Original frameworks
- Customer observations
- Lessons learned
- Internal experiments
- Contrarian viewpoints
- Implementation guidance

Without original contribution, the content risks becoming another summary of existing ideas.

---

### Step 4 — Evaluate Strategic Alignment

Validate the topic against company priorities.

| Decision Gate | Yes / No |
|--------------|----------|
| Does this support our positioning? | □ |
| Does it answer a recurring customer question? | □ |
| Does it strengthen long-term authority? | □ |
| Can we contribute something original? | □ |
| Is this useful independently of our product? | □ |

Only ideas passing every checkpoint proceed to production.

---

## Quality Gate

Before writing begins, confirm:

- [ ] Customer problem clearly defined.
- [ ] Educational outcome documented.
- [ ] Original contribution identified.
- [ ] Strategic alignment confirmed.
- [ ] Supporting evidence available.
- [ ] No duplicate content already exists.

---

## Common Failure Modes

The following issues indicate that an idea requires further validation:

- Chasing trending topics without customer relevance.
- Repeating advice already published by competitors.
- Writing product-focused content disguised as education.
- Using AI-generated topic suggestions without customer validation.
- Selecting ideas because they are easy to create rather than valuable to the audience.

---

## Success Criteria

This SOP is successful when:

- Every published topic has a documented customer problem.
- Every idea contributes a differentiated perspective.
- The editorial team understands why the topic deserves publication.
- Weak ideas are rejected before production begins.

---

## My Notes

During this research, I realised that content quality is largely determined before the first sentence is written.

Strong writing cannot rescue weak strategy.

For that reason, I intentionally placed idea validation before content creation in the operating model.

# SOP 03 — Creating the First Draft

## Objective

Transform a validated content opportunity into a first draft that reflects customer understanding, original thinking and strategic positioning.

The objective is not to produce a publish-ready document.

The objective is to capture the strongest ideas before editorial refinement begins.

---

## My Decision

I decided that content creation should begin with human thinking and customer evidence before AI enters the workflow.

AI should improve execution efficiency.

It should not become the source of strategic insight.

This distinction became one of the strongest recurring themes throughout the research and directly influenced how AI is used throughout this playbook.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some organisations now use AI to generate complete drafts with minimal human input.

This approach increases publishing speed.

However, it also increases the likelihood of generic positioning and repetitive messaging.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I deliberately rejected AI-first drafting because none of the practitioners demonstrated that automation alone creates stronger customer trust.

Instead, they consistently emphasised combining AI efficiency with human expertise.

---

## Execution Steps

### Step 1

Document the customer problem.

### Step 2

Write the core argument without AI assistance.

### Step 3

List supporting evidence, examples and customer observations.

### Step 4

Use AI to improve clarity, structure and readability.

### Step 5

Review every AI-generated suggestion against the original strategic objective.

Human judgement remains the final decision-maker.

---

## Quality Gate

Before editorial review:

- [ ] Original insight included.
- [ ] Customer problem addressed.
- [ ] Educational value exceeds promotional messaging.
- [ ] AI output reviewed manually.
- [ ] Final draft reflects company positioning.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- Allowing AI to generate strategic positioning.
- Publishing generic frameworks without original interpretation.
- Over-editing until the author's perspective disappears.
- Prioritising speed over clarity.

---

## Success Criteria

The first draft should clearly demonstrate:

- Original thinking.
- Practical educational value.
- Strategic consistency.
- Human judgement.
- Readiness for editorial review.

---

## My Notes

Throughout this project, I found no convincing evidence that AI replaces strategic marketing judgement.

Instead, the strongest practitioners consistently used AI to remove repetitive work while investing more time in customer understanding, positioning and decision-making.

That principle remains unchanged throughout this playbook.

# SOP 04 — Editorial Review & Quality Assurance

## Objective

Ensure that every published asset reflects the strategic decisions documented in this repository rather than simply meeting grammatical or stylistic standards.

Editorial review is the final opportunity to improve strategic quality before content reaches the audience.

The purpose of this SOP is not to make content "sound better."

Its purpose is to verify that the content deserves to be published.

---

## My Decision

I decided that editorial review should evaluate strategic quality before writing quality.

Grammar mistakes can be corrected.

Weak positioning, poor customer understanding and generic insights are significantly more difficult to fix once content has been published.

For that reason, editorial review should function as a strategic checkpoint rather than a proofreading exercise.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some organisations treat editorial review primarily as:

- grammar correction
- formatting
- brand voice alignment
- proofreading

Although these tasks remain important, they occur too late to improve weak strategic thinking.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I deliberately expanded editorial review beyond copyediting because I found no evidence that technically polished content consistently produces stronger business outcomes when the underlying idea is weak.

Quality should be evaluated before presentation.

---

## Execution Steps

### Step 1

Confirm that the customer problem is immediately clear.

---

### Step 2

Verify that the content teaches something useful rather than repeating commonly available advice.

---

### Step 3

Identify the author's original contribution.

If no original contribution exists, return the draft for revision.

---

### Step 4

Review factual accuracy.

Verify every claim, statistic and example.

---

### Step 5

Review clarity, structure and readability.

Only after strategic quality has been approved should stylistic edits begin.

---

## Quality Gate

Before approval, confirm:

- [ ] Customer problem clearly defined.
- [ ] Educational value exceeds promotional messaging.
- [ ] Original insight clearly visible.
- [ ] Claims supported by evidence.
- [ ] AI-generated sections manually reviewed.
- [ ] Content reflects company positioning.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- Editing language instead of improving ideas.
- Publishing because deadlines exist.
- Removing the author's personality during editing.
- Treating AI-generated drafts as finished work.

---

## Success Criteria

Editorial review is complete when the content:

- strengthens trust,
- demonstrates expertise,
- communicates a differentiated perspective,
- and aligns with the operating model established in Documents 02 and 03.

---

## My Notes

I intentionally designed editorial review as a decision-making activity rather than a writing activity.

This is one of the strongest quality controls in the entire operating system.

# SOP 05 — Publishing & Distribution

## Objective

Maximise the value of every published content asset through intentional distribution.

Publishing marks the beginning of audience interaction rather than the end of content creation.

---

## My Decision

I decided that distribution planning should be completed before publication.

Waiting until after publishing introduces unnecessary inconsistency and significantly reduces the return on the effort invested in creating high-quality content.

Ignoring it introduces unnecessary risk into an otherwise strong content system.

---

## Execution Steps

Before publication:

1. Final approval completed.
2. Publishing schedule confirmed.
3. Founder or SME informed.
4. Employee advocacy opportunities identified.
5. Repurposing opportunities documented.
6. Success metrics defined.

---

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Publishing time confirmed.
- [ ] Supporting assets complete.
- [ ] Distribution plan documented.
- [ ] Employee sharing prepared.
- [ ] Repurposing backlog created.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- Publishing without a distribution plan.
- Measuring success only through impressions.
- Treating LinkedIn as the only distribution channel.
- Ignoring opportunities to repurpose successful content.

---

## Success Criteria

Successful execution means:

- the intended audience discovers the content,
- multiple distribution opportunities are utilised,
- and the content continues creating value after publication.

---

## My Notes

Publishing completes the production process.

Distribution begins the learning process.

Treating those as separate activities weakens the operating system.

# SOP 06 — Performance Review & Continuous Improvement

## Objective

Convert content performance into better future decisions.

The objective of measurement is not reporting.

The objective is learning.

---

## My Decision

I decided that every publishing cycle should end with a structured retrospective.

Without deliberate reflection, content teams repeatedly optimise execution while failing to improve decision-making.

# Appendix A — Content Review Scorecard

Every content asset should be evaluated using the same scorecard before publication.

| Category | Question | Score (1–5) |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Customer Relevance | Does this solve a genuine customer problem? | |
| Educational Value | Will the audience learn something practical? | |
| Original Thinking | Does this contain a perspective competitors are unlikely to publish? | |
| Strategic Alignment | Does it strengthen company positioning? | |
| Trust Building | Does it build credibility before promotion? | |
| Evidence Quality | Are claims supported by reliable evidence or real experience? | |
| Distribution Readiness | Has a distribution plan been prepared? | |
| AI Quality Control | Has every AI-generated contribution been reviewed manually? | |

---

## Decision Rule

**36–40**

Publish immediately.

**30–35**

Minor revisions required.

**24–29**

Return to editorial review.

**Below 24**

Return to strategy.

Do not publish.

---

# Why I Included This Scorecard

Throughout my research I noticed that experienced practitioners consistently relied on judgment rather than rigid formulas.

However, judgment becomes difficult to scale as teams grow.

The purpose of this scorecard is therefore **not** to replace judgment.

It is to make judgment more consistent across different reviewers.

Rather than asking

> "Do I like this content?"

the reviewer asks

> "Does this content satisfy the operating decisions documented throughout this repository?"

That distinction keeps editorial decisions aligned with strategy rather than individual preference.

# Closing Reflection

A playbook is only valuable if it helps people make better decisions.

Throughout this repository I deliberately avoided documenting isolated tactics.

Instead, I focused on documenting the reasoning behind those tactics.

Processes will evolve.

AI capabilities will improve.

LinkedIn will change.

Customer expectations will continue to shift.

For that reason, I do not expect every workflow in this playbook to remain unchanged.

I do expect the decision-making framework behind those workflows to remain valuable because it encourages marketers to evaluate evidence, question assumptions and continuously improve rather than simply repeat existing practices.

If I joined a B2B SaaS marketing team tomorrow, this is the operating system I would feel confident implementing—not because I believe it is perfect, but because every major decision is transparent, evidence-informed and open to revision as new evidence becomes available.
