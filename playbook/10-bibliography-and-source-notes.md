# 10 – Research References

> ## Why this document exists
>
> Every recommendation documented throughout this repository is based on the research I conducted for this project rather than on unsupported personal opinion.
>
> From the beginning of this project, one of my objectives was to ensure that every meaningful recommendation could be traced back to the practitioner evidence that informed it.
>
> Rather than collecting references only after completing the repository, I documented sources alongside my research and referred back to them whenever I made a strategic decision, compared conflicting viewpoints or developed my own conclusions.
>
> This reference document serves four purposes:
>
> 1. It provides a complete record of the practitioner sources reviewed while developing this operating model.
> 2. It allows readers to trace the reasoning behind major recommendations back to the original practitioner material that informed them.
> 3. It distinguishes practitioner evidence from my own interpretation, synthesis and original ideas documented throughout the repository.
> 4. It makes the research process transparent so that readers can understand not only *what* conclusions I reached, but also *how* I reached them.
>
> I intentionally organised the references by practitioner rather than by repository document.
>
> Most strategic decisions documented throughout this repository were influenced by multiple sources from the same practitioner rather than by a single article, newsletter or podcast episode.
>
> Organising the references in this way better reflects how I actually conducted the research and makes it easier to understand the broader body of work that influenced each decision.

---

# Research Scope

This repository focuses specifically on practitioner knowledge within modern B2B SaaS marketing.

The research was intentionally limited to practitioners who regularly publish original thinking through newsletters, LinkedIn, podcasts, interviews and other long-form educational content.

I selected practitioners whose combined work covered the major disciplines required to build the operating model documented throughout this repository, including:

- Positioning
- Messaging
- LinkedIn Organic Content
- Demand Generation
- Category Creation
- AI-Assisted Marketing
- Executive Thought Leadership
- Go-to-Market Strategy
- Marketing Operations

The objective of this research was not to identify a single "best" practitioner.

Instead, I compared different practitioner perspectives, identified recurring patterns, examined areas of disagreement and then documented the decisions I made while building this operating model.

Throughout the repository, I intentionally separated three different stages of reasoning:

- **Evidence** — recommendations collected from practitioner research.
- **Interpretation** — my analysis of recurring patterns, assumptions and disagreements across the sources reviewed.
- **Decision** — the operating principles, frameworks and recommendations I ultimately chose to include in this repository.

Maintaining that distinction helped ensure that readers could differentiate between established practitioner recommendations and my own evidence-informed conclusions.

---

# A Note on Citation Quality

Whenever possible, I referenced original practitioner material rather than secondary summaries, reposts or commentary.

Where similar recommendations appeared across multiple sources from the same practitioner, I reviewed those sources together before drawing conclusions instead of relying on a single article or post.

When multiple practitioners reached similar conclusions independently, my confidence in those recommendations increased.

When practitioners disagreed, I compared the assumptions behind each recommendation before deciding whether it belonged within the operating model developed throughout this repository.

This approach reflects the research methodology documented in **Document 01** and the evidence-informed decision-making process applied consistently throughout the repository.

# Source Limitations

While every effort was made to rely on original practitioner material, this repository has several limitations.

First, the research is based primarily on publicly available newsletters, LinkedIn posts, podcasts, interviews and videos rather than internal company documentation or proprietary performance data.

Second, practitioner recommendations are naturally influenced by the organisations, audiences and commercial environments in which they operate. A recommendation that performs well in one context may require adaptation before being applied elsewhere.

Third, many recommendations represent informed professional judgement rather than experimentally validated research. Throughout this repository, I therefore treated practitioner content as evidence to evaluate rather than facts to adopt uncritically.

For that reason, I compared multiple practitioners, looked for recurring patterns and documented where I intentionally adapted or rejected recommendations while building this operating model.

# Closing Note

This repository is not intended to be a collection of universal best practices.

It is a transparent record of how I researched practitioner content, evaluated competing recommendations and made evidence-informed decisions while developing a LinkedIn Organic Content Operating Model for B2B SaaS.

Where multiple practitioners independently reached similar conclusions, my confidence in those recommendations increased.

Where practitioners disagreed, I compared the assumptions behind their advice before deciding whether those recommendations fit the operating model documented throughout this repository.

My objective was never to identify one "correct" practitioner.

Instead, it was to develop an operating model that remains transparent about both its evidence and the reasoning behind every major decision.

I hope this reference section enables readers to trace every significant recommendation back to its original source while also understanding the judgement that connected those sources into a single, coherent operating model.
