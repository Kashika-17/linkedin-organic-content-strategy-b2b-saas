# Decision Log

> ## Purpose
>
> This document records the major research and design decisions made while developing this repository.
>
> Rather than documenting every edit, it focuses on decisions that materially influenced the final operating model.
>
> Each decision includes:
>
> - the problem being addressed,
> - the alternatives considered,
> - the practitioner evidence reviewed,
> - the final decision,
> - the rationale for that decision,
> - and the repository documents affected.
>
> The objective is to make the reasoning behind the repository transparent and auditable.

# Decision D-01

## Problem

What should serve as the primary evidence base for this repository?

## Alternatives Considered

- Generic marketing blogs
- AI-generated summaries
- Practitioner research

## Evidence Reviewed

Newsletters, LinkedIn posts, podcasts, interviews and videos from the ten practitioners documented in **Document 10**.

## Final Decision

Use original practitioner research as the primary evidence source.

## Why

Original practitioner material provides direct access to recommendations, reasoning and implementation experience without introducing an additional layer of interpretation.

This also allows conflicting viewpoints to be compared directly.

## Repository Impact

This decision influenced every document in the repository.

# Decision D-02

## Problem

How should the repository be organised?

## Alternatives Considered

- One document per practitioner
- Chronological research notes
- Operating principles

## Evidence Reviewed

Comparison of recurring themes across all practitioner research.

## Final Decision

Organise the repository around operating principles rather than individual experts.

## Why

Most important recommendations appeared across multiple practitioners.

Organising the repository by decision area made it easier to compare evidence and explain why particular recommendations were adopted.

## Repository Impact

Documents 02–05.

---

# Decision D-03

## Problem

How should recommendations be presented?

## Alternatives Considered

- Summarise practitioner advice
- Present conclusions only
- Separate evidence, interpretation and decisions

## Evidence Reviewed

Patterns identified while comparing practitioner recommendations.

## Final Decision

Separate practitioner evidence, personal interpretation and final decisions throughout the repository.

## Why

Readers should be able to distinguish between what practitioners recommended and the conclusions I reached after comparing those recommendations.

## Repository Impact

Entire repository.

---

# Decision D-04

## Problem

How should conflicting recommendations be evaluated?

## Alternatives Considered

- Follow the most well-known practitioner
- Average all recommendations
- Compare recurring patterns and underlying assumptions

## Evidence Reviewed

Areas where practitioners agreed and disagreed.

## Final Decision

Prioritise recurring patterns while documenting meaningful disagreements.

## Why

Independent agreement across multiple practitioners increased confidence.

Disagreements often reflected differences in organisational context rather than errors.

## Repository Impact

Documents 02, 05 and 08.

---

# Decision D-05

## Problem

Should practitioner disagreements be simplified into a single recommendation?

## Alternatives Considered

- Present one "best" answer
- Preserve differing viewpoints

## Evidence Reviewed

Recommendations related to positioning, AI adoption, executive branding, GTM strategy and content distribution.

## Final Decision

Preserve practitioner disagreement.

## Why

Understanding *why* practitioners disagree provides more useful guidance than presenting a single universal recommendation.

## Repository Impact

Document 05.

# Decision D-06

## Problem

How should AI be positioned within the operating model?

## Alternatives Considered

- AI generates content independently
- AI assists research and execution
- Minimal AI usage

## Evidence Reviewed

Practitioner recommendations concerning AI workflows together with observations recorded during the research process.

## Final Decision

Position AI as a supporting capability rather than a replacement for strategic judgement.

## Why

AI improved efficiency throughout the project, but practitioner comparison, interpretation and final recommendations required human judgement.

## Repository Impact

Documents 02, 04 and 08.

---

# Decision D-07

## Problem

How should practitioners be evaluated?

## Alternatives Considered

- Rank practitioners
- Recommend or reject practitioners
- Evaluate recommendation transferability

## Evidence Reviewed

Comparative analysis documented throughout Documents 01–09.

## Final Decision

Evaluate recommendations according to transferability rather than perceived expertise.

## Why

A recommendation can be valuable while still depending on organisational conditions that are not universally present.

## Repository Impact

Document 09.

# Decision D-08

## Problem

Should repository limitations be documented?

## Alternatives Considered

- Present the operating model as complete
- Explicitly acknowledge limitations

## Evidence Reviewed

Scope of practitioner research and assumptions identified throughout the project.

## Final Decision

Document limitations and identify where future evidence may change conclusions.

## Why

Transparent research should explain both confidence and uncertainty.

## Repository Impact

Document 08.

# Decision D-09

## Problem

How should evidence be referenced?

## Alternatives Considered

- Reference only key sources
- Cite every recommendation

## Evidence Reviewed

Assignment requirements and research methodology.

## Final Decision

Ensure every recommendation is traceable to practitioner evidence.

## Why

Traceability improves transparency, allows independent verification and strengthens confidence in the conclusions presented.

## Repository Impact

Entire repository, especially Documents 01 and 10.

# Decision D-10

## Problem

What should the final deliverable represent?

## Alternatives Considered

- Collection of practitioner summaries
- Collection of best practices
- Evidence-informed operating model

## Evidence Reviewed

Research conducted across all practitioner sources.

## Final Decision

Develop an operating model that documents both evidence and decision-making.

## Why

The objective of the assignment was not only to collect information but also to demonstrate judgement.

An operating model better reflects the process of evaluating evidence, comparing perspectives and making transparent decisions.

## Repository Impact

Entire repository.

# Closing Reflection

Completing this repository changed how I think about practitioner research.

At the beginning of the project, I assumed the most important task would be identifying the best recommendations.

By the end of the project, I realised the more valuable skill was explaining **why** one recommendation belonged in this operating model while another required adaptation or was excluded.

Every major decision recorded in this document can be traced to practitioner evidence, compared against alternative approaches and justified within the context of the operating model developed throughout this repository.

That, more than any individual recommendation, is the outcome I will take forward into future research and strategic work.
