# Decision 01 — Optimise for Trust Before Reach

## My Decision

If I were responsible for LinkedIn Organic Content at a B2B SaaS company, I would optimise the content strategy for **building trust before maximising reach**.

I deliberately chose this approach because trust compounds over time, whereas reach is largely influenced by platform behaviour, distribution and timing.

A post with limited reach can still strengthen credibility with the right audience.

A viral post that fails to build trust rarely creates long-term business value.

For that reason, trust became the first operating decision in this playbook.

## Why I Accepted This Evidence

Although these practitioners work in different areas of B2B SaaS marketing, they independently arrived at a similar conclusion:

Trust should be treated as an asset that compounds over time.

None of them positioned LinkedIn primarily as a direct-response acquisition channel.

Instead, they consistently described it as a platform for reducing uncertainty before buying intent exists.

That recurring pattern increased my confidence that this recommendation represents a durable marketing principle rather than a temporary platform tactic.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some practitioners and creators argue that the primary objective of LinkedIn content should be maximising visibility through:

- high posting frequency
- trend participation
- algorithm optimisation
- engagement-first content

This perspective assumes that increased visibility naturally produces more opportunities.

While visibility is important, I found limited evidence that visibility alone consistently produces stronger business outcomes without corresponding credibility.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I chose not to optimise primarily for reach because:

- reach is heavily influenced by platform behaviour
- algorithm changes remain outside the marketer's control
- visibility without credibility rarely creates sustainable authority

Instead, I view reach as an amplifier.

Trust determines whether amplification creates business value.

---

## Implementation

If I were implementing this decision, I would evaluate every content idea using one question before writing begins.

> **"Will this increase trust even if it receives very little engagement?"**

If the answer is no, the idea should be revised before entering production.

This simple decision gate prevents the content calendar from becoming driven by trends rather than customer value.

---

## Risks & Limitations

This approach intentionally sacrifices some short-term visibility.

Companies seeking immediate lead generation may prefer a more promotion-focused strategy.

However, for organisations investing in long-term brand equity, I believe this trade-off is justified.

The effectiveness of this decision also depends on maintaining publishing consistency over an extended period.

---

## My Conclusion

After reviewing the available evidence, I believe trust should be treated as the primary optimisation objective for LinkedIn Organic Content.

Reach remains valuable.

But reach should amplify trust—not replace it.

# Decision 02 — Optimise for Educational Value Instead of Promotional Messaging

## My Decision

I decided that educational content should become the default publishing standard throughout the operating model.

Promotion remains necessary.

However, promotion should occur after expertise has been demonstrated rather than before.

In other words:

> **Teach first. Sell later.**

This decision influences every workflow contained in the remainder of this repository.

---

---

### Dave Gerhardt

**Title:** Content Distribution, Creator Partnerships & Media Strategy

**Source:** LinkedIn Post  
**Date:** 12 May 2026

https://www.linkedin.com/posts/devinreed_you-shouldnt-write-like-a-journalist-analyst-activity-7459984518205136896-Cw5c

Rather than encouraging product promotion, Devin focuses on storytelling that helps readers understand problems more clearly.

---

## Why I Accepted This Evidence

Although these practitioners use different formats and communication styles, they consistently prioritise helping the audience over persuading the audience.

That distinction became one of the strongest recurring themes across the research.

Educational content builds authority.

Promotional content spends authority.

Because authority compounds over time, I believe education should become the default operating model.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some marketing teams intentionally maintain a high proportion of promotional content because it appears easier to connect directly to pipeline generation.

This approach can produce short-term commercial results.

However, it also increases the likelihood of audience fatigue when educational value is limited.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I rejected promotion-first content as the default strategy because it depends heavily on existing brand trust.

Companies without established credibility often struggle to sustain engagement when content repeatedly focuses on products instead of customer problems.

---

## Implementation

Before approving any content, I would ask:

> **"Would this still be useful if every product mention were removed?"**

If the answer is yes, the content provides independent value.

If the answer is no, it should be revised until educational value becomes the primary outcome.

---

## Risks & Limitations

Educational content usually compounds more slowly than promotional campaigns.

Teams under significant short-term revenue pressure may find this approach difficult to maintain without executive alignment.

For that reason, educational content should complement commercial campaigns rather than replace them entirely.

---

## My Conclusion

After evaluating the evidence, I concluded that educational value should become the default publishing standard because it consistently strengthens trust, authority and long-term demand generation across multiple independent practitioners.

# Decision 03 — Prioritise Original Thinking Over Publishing Frequency

## My Decision

After reviewing the research, I decided that publishing **fewer, higher-quality posts containing original thinking** is a more sustainable strategy than optimising primarily for publishing frequency.

I am not rejecting consistency.

I am rejecting the assumption that frequency alone creates authority.

The objective of LinkedIn Organic Content should be to become a trusted source of insight, not simply a familiar name appearing in the feed.

Publishing more content increases opportunities for visibility.

Publishing better content increases reasons for people to return.

For long-term B2B SaaS growth, I believe the second outcome is significantly more valuable.

https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/build-marketing-strategy-skill-in-claude-code

Emily demonstrates that AI is most effective when combined with strong strategic inputs rather than used as a substitute for thinking.

---

## Why I Accepted This Evidence

Although these practitioners discuss different topics, they converge on one important principle:

Technology is making content production easier.

It is **not** making original thinking easier.

As AI-generated content becomes more common, unique interpretation becomes increasingly valuable.

That convinced me to optimise for differentiated thinking rather than publishing cadence.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

A common recommendation within creator communities is:

- publish every day
- maximise consistency
- let volume improve quality

This approach assumes that increased publishing frequency naturally increases audience growth.

It can be effective for creator-led businesses where visibility itself is the objective.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I chose not to adopt a frequency-first strategy because I found little evidence that posting more often consistently improves business outcomes for B2B SaaS companies.

Most practitioners I researched evaluated success through trust, authority and customer understanding rather than post volume.

I therefore consider publishing frequency an execution decision rather than a strategic objective.

---

## Implementation

Instead of defining success as "five posts per week", I would define success as:

- Every post contributes one original observation.
- Every post answers a meaningful customer question.
- Every post teaches something that competitors are unlikely to publish.
- Every post reflects direct experience, customer insight or implementation learning.

Publishing cadence should support these standards—not replace them.

---

## Risks & Limitations

Publishing less frequently may reduce short-term visibility.

It also requires stronger editorial discipline because every published asset carries greater importance.

Teams without a consistent research process may struggle to sustain originality over time.

---

## My Conclusion

I concluded that originality creates stronger competitive advantage than publishing frequency.

Consistency remains important.

However, consistency should describe the quality of thinking—not merely the number of posts published.

# Decision 04 — Use AI as an Execution Multiplier, Not a Strategic Decision Maker

## My Decision

I decided to position AI as an execution capability throughout this playbook rather than as the primary source of strategy.

AI should accelerate research, drafting, analysis and repurposing.

It should not determine positioning, customer understanding or editorial judgment.

Those decisions require business context, market understanding and human accountability.

## Why I Accepted This Evidence

Across the AI-focused practitioners, I found broad agreement on one principle.

The competitive advantage does not come from having access to AI.

It comes from knowing where AI should—and should not—be used.

This distinction significantly influenced how AI appears throughout the remainder of this repository.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Some organisations are attempting to automate large portions of their content process using AI with minimal human involvement.

This approach promises higher efficiency and lower production costs.

However, it also increases the likelihood of producing generic content that lacks differentiation and customer insight.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I deliberately rejected an AI-first operating model because none of the practitioners presented convincing evidence that automation alone creates stronger positioning or more trusted brands.

The research consistently suggests that AI amplifies existing strategic capability.

It does not replace it.

---

## Implementation

Within this operating model, AI should support:

- research synthesis
- transcript summarisation
- draft generation
- content repurposing
- workflow automation
- performance analysis

Humans remain responsible for:

- positioning
- strategic decisions
- customer understanding
- editorial approval
- final publishing decisions

This separation preserves efficiency without sacrificing originality.

---

## Risks & Limitations

As AI capabilities improve, some execution boundaries described here may change.

However, I expect strategic judgment, customer empathy and business context to remain fundamentally human responsibilities for the foreseeable future.

This decision should therefore be reviewed periodically as technology evolves.

---

## My Conclusion

I concluded that AI should strengthen marketing systems rather than replace marketing judgment.

The organisations that benefit most from AI will not be those that automate everything.

They will be those that automate routine work while investing more time in better thinking.

# Decision 05 — Treat Distribution as Part of Content Creation, Not a Separate Activity

## My Decision

I decided that distribution should be planned before content is created rather than after it is published.

Many content teams treat publishing as the finish line.

After reviewing the research, I believe publishing is only the midpoint of the content lifecycle.

The value of a strong piece of content is determined not only by its quality, but also by how intentionally it is distributed to the people it was created for.

For this reason, every content asset in this playbook includes a distribution plan before writing begins.

---

## Evidence Considered

### Ross Simmonds

**Title:** Create Once, Distribute Forever  
**Source:** YouTube  
**Date:** 09 Dec 2024

https://youtu.be/7_bFP2iVVN0

Ross repeatedly argues that companies underinvest in distribution while overinvesting in content creation. His central thesis is that distribution should be designed into the workflow rather than treated as an afterthought.

### Ross Simmonds

https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/rss-55-reddit-ai-distribution-the-b2b-playbook-most/id1653650073

Ross expands this idea by showing how content should be adapted for different discovery channels instead of relying on a single publishing destination.

---

### Dave Gerhardt

**Title:** Content Distribution, Creator Partnerships & Media Strategy  
**Source:** YouTube  
**Date:** 30 Apr 2024

https://youtu.be/YgzdkcHltCE

Dave encourages marketers to think like media operators, where creating valuable content and ensuring it reaches the intended audience are equally important responsibilities.

---

## Why I Accepted This Evidence

Although Ross focuses primarily on distribution and Dave approaches the topic from a media strategy perspective, both reach the same conclusion.

Content without distribution creates significantly less business value than content supported by an intentional promotion strategy.

The consistency of this recommendation across multiple practitioners convinced me that distribution should become a mandatory stage of the operating model rather than an optional marketing activity.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

A common assumption is that high-quality content naturally attracts attention through platform algorithms.

This approach prioritises improving the content itself while placing relatively little emphasis on structured distribution.

Although exceptional content occasionally succeeds without active promotion, I found limited evidence that this produces reliable business outcomes over time.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I rejected a publish-and-wait approach because it places too much responsibility on external platform algorithms.

Distribution is one of the few variables marketers can actively influence.

Ignoring it unnecessarily reduces the return on the effort invested in content creation.

---

## Implementation

Every content brief should answer the following questions before production begins:

- Which audience is this created for?
- Which distribution channels will be used?
- How will employees or founders amplify it?
- Can the asset be repurposed into additional formats?
- How will success be measured?

Only after these questions have been answered should content move into production.

---

## Risks & Limitations

Planning distribution increases the effort required before publication.

Smaller teams with limited resources may initially struggle to execute multi-channel distribution consistently.

In those situations, I would prioritise fewer assets with stronger distribution rather than increasing publishing frequency.

---

## My Conclusion

I concluded that distribution is not a post-publication activity.

It is an integral part of content strategy and should be planned with the same level of discipline as content creation itself.

# Decision 06 — Build a Learning System Instead of Chasing Individual Wins

## My Decision

The final decision that shaped this playbook was to optimise for continuous learning rather than isolated content success.

A single successful LinkedIn post provides temporary visibility.

A repeatable system that learns from every post creates long-term competitive advantage.

For this reason, every workflow in this repository ends with structured review rather than publication.

None present success as the result of a single tactic.

Instead, they repeatedly emphasise experimentation, measurement and refinement.

That pattern convinced me to design this playbook as a learning system rather than a collection of static best practices.

---

## Alternative Viewpoints

Many organisations evaluate content primarily through individual post performance.

Metrics such as impressions, likes and follower growth often become the primary indicators of success.

While these metrics provide useful feedback, they do not necessarily explain why content succeeded or how future performance can be improved.

---

## Why I Rejected the Alternative

I chose not to evaluate success solely through engagement metrics because they measure outcomes rather than learning.

A post with modest engagement may generate valuable customer insights.

Conversely, a highly visible post may contribute very little to long-term positioning or business growth.

For that reason, I believe learning should become the primary output of the measurement process.

---

## Implementation

Every publishing cycle should conclude with a structured retrospective.

The review should answer:

- What assumptions were validated?
- What surprised us?
- What did customers respond to?
- What should change before the next publishing cycle?
- Which recommendations in this playbook should be updated?

This ensures that the operating model continues improving rather than remaining static.

---

## Risks & Limitations

Building a learning system requires discipline and documentation.

Teams focused exclusively on short-term campaign execution may struggle to invest sufficient time in structured retrospectives.

Without leadership support, this process can gradually become inconsistent.

---

## My Conclusion

I concluded that the greatest long-term advantage does not come from producing one exceptional piece of content.

It comes from building an operating system that becomes more effective after every publishing cycle.

That belief shapes every workflow contained in the remainder of this repository.

# Closing Reflection

The six decisions documented in this file form the strategic foundation of the entire playbook.

They are intentionally interconnected.

- Building trust influences what content is created.
- Prioritising education shapes how ideas are communicated.
- Original thinking determines differentiation.
- Responsible AI adoption improves execution without replacing judgment.
- Distribution maximises the value of every published asset.
- Continuous learning ensures the system improves over time.

More importantly, these decisions were not selected because they were popular.

They were selected because they remained the most transferable after comparing multiple practitioners, evaluating conflicting viewpoints and considering the trade-offs associated with each approach.

That distinction is central to this repository.

The objective was never to identify a universally correct LinkedIn strategy.

The objective was to develop an operating model whose decisions remain defensible, transparent and adaptable as marketing, technology and buyer behaviour continue to evolve.
